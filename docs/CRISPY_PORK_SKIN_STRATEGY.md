# ERN Crispy Pork Skin Strategy

## Origin
The user's favorite noodle-shop story is the product metaphor: the noodle soup is good already, but the small crispy-pork-skin addition has an outsized effect. Without it, the whole experience feels less special.

ERN should use the same principle: a few small, distinctive layers can make the entire product feel memorable and lovable, without turning it into a heavy feature collection.

## What counts as “crispy pork skin” in ERN
1. **ERN Guide** — a friendly tour guide that breaks the ice and helps people understand, discover and move through ERN.
2. **Now Moments** — temporary visitor expression: structured signals first, then temporary photos/short clips after safety infrastructure is real.
3. **Local Earth** — intentional discovery of small places and small businesses through Search, Atlas and Guide.
4. **Truthful window variety** — livestreams, quasi-live refreshed imagery and continuously updated current photos all have value when labeled correctly.
5. **Serendipity** — small surprises, local context, good timing, “why now,” and unexpected nearby discoveries.
6. **Useful next actions** — after someone becomes interested in a place, ERN can help them decide what to do next without letting commercial partners control ranking.

## Product test
Before adding a new layer, ask:
- Does it create disproportionate visitor value?
- Does it make ERN more human, memorable or useful?
- Is it lightweight?
- Does it preserve truth and trust?
- Does it strengthen the whole journey rather than duplicate an existing function?

If not, it is not “crispy pork skin”; it is just more software.

## Important boundary
This strategy does **not** mean endless micro-polish. It means choosing a small number of high-effect touches that amplify the complete ERN experience.
